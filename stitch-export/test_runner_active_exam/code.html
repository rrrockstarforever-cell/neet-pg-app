Q10. Regarding glycopeptide resistance mechanisms in Enterococci, which of the following statements is/are correct?

1

The VanA phenotype confers high-level resistance to both vancomycin and teicoplanin.

2

The VanB phenotype confers vancomycin resistance while teicoplanin often remains active.

3

The VanC phenotype is intrinsic to Enterococcus gallinarum and Enterococcus casseliflavus and gives low-level vancomycin resistance.

4

The VanA gene cluster is chromosomally encoded in Enterococcus faecium.

Confidence: High (100%)
check
Correct Response: Option B (1, 2 and 3 only) 82% of INI-CET top-centile candidates got this right.
verified_user Faculty Verified

clinical_notes Diagnostic Rationale & Phenotypic Dissection

Statement 1: Correct VanA

The VanA phenotype yields substitution of the terminal D-Ala-D-Ala cell wall peptidoglycan target with D-Ala-D-Lac, reducing vancomycin binding affinity ~1,000-fold. It confers inducible, high-level resistance to both vancomycin (MIC ≥ 64 μg/mL) and teicoplanin (MIC ≥ 16 μg/mL).

Statement 2: Correct VanB

The VanB phenotype is inducible strictly by vancomycin, not teicoplanin. Hence, teicoplanin susceptibility is clinically retained in vitro, although breakout mutations can emerge during monotherapy.

Statement 3: Correct VanC (Intrinsic)

VanC is intrinsic, chromosomal, non-transferable resistance seen specifically in motile enterococci (E. gallinarum, E. casseliflavus). It utilizes a D-Ala-D-Ser ligase causing low-level resistance (MIC 2–32 μg/mL). Teicoplanin remains universally susceptible.

Statement 4: Incorrect Trap Point

The VanA resistance determinant is carried on the Tn1546 transposon located primarily on conjugative plasmids (readily transferable to Staphylococcus aureus creating VRSA), not inherently chromosomal.
