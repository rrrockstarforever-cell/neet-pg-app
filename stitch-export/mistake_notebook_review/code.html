VanA vs. VanB Phenotypic Glycopeptide Resistance in Enterococcus faecium

A 54-year-old critically ill ICU patient with central line-associated bacteremia is found to have vancomycin-resistant Enterococcus faecium. Genomic profiling reveals the VanA operon. Which of the following statements regarding the genetic transfer and phenotypic resistance profile of VanA vs. VanB is accurate?

close Your Selected Choice Option D

"VanA resistance is chromosomal; conveys resistance exclusively to Teicoplanin while sparing Vancomycin."

check_circle Correct Answer Option B

"VanA is transposon-mediated (Tn1546), inducible, and confers dual high-level resistance to both Vancomycin and Teicoplanin."

psychology_alt
Trap Type: Transposon vs Chromosome Translocation Pitfall

You committed a classical Board Distractor error. VanB is chromosome- or plasmid-borne and confers resistance to Vancomycin but remains susceptible to Teicoplanin. VanA confers resistance to both via substitution of D-Ala-D-Ala with D-Ala-D-Lac.

Mechanism: The VanA gene cluster is localized on transposon Tn1546. It alters the peptidoglycan cell wall precursor by producing D-alanyl-D-lactate instead of D-alanyl-D-alanine, leading to a 1,000-fold decrease in binding affinity for glycopeptides.

Reference: Harrison's Internal Medicine 21e, Ch. 142 (Enterococcal Infections, p. 1184) • Yield Index: 94% INI-CET/NEET-PG Match

Pauci-Immune Crescentic Glomerulonephritis (Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis)

A 42-year-old male presents with hemoptysis, recurrent sinusitis, acute oliguria, and serum creatinine 4.8 mg/dL. Renal core biopsy stained with Silver Methenamine reveals cellular crescent formation compressing the glomerular capillary tuft in >65% of glomeruli. Immunofluorescence shows faint, non-specific immunoglobulin deposition.

H&E / Silver Stain
close Selected Distractor Option A

"Goodpasture Syndrome: Linear IgG along basement membrane"

check_circle Correct Answer Option C

"GPA / c-ANCA (PR3) Pauci-Immune RPGN Type III"

image_aspect_ratio
Trap Type: Image Distractor Ambiguity • IF Pattern Overlook

You latched onto hemoptysis + renal failure and jumped to Anti-GBM (Goodpasture). However, the stem specified "faint, non-specific immunoglobulin deposition" (Pauci-Immune). Goodpasture requires smooth linear IgG ribbon deposition.

Sequencing of Adrenergic Blockade in Pheochromocytoma Resection

A 38-year-old woman with severe paroxysmal hypertension, palpitations, and elevated 24-hour urinary metanephrines is scheduled for adrenalectomy. Blood pressure is 198/112 mmHg with heart rate 124 bpm. Which pharmacological sequence is mandatory before operating?

close Selected Distractor Option B

"Initiate Propranolol or Atenolol immediately to control severe reflex tachycardia, then titrate Phenoxybenzamine."

check_circle Correct Answer Option D

"Alpha-blockade FIRST with Phenoxybenzamine for 10-14 days; Beta-blockers added ONLY after effective alpha blockade is established."

crisis_alert
Trap Type: Unopposed Alpha-1 Vasoconstriction Lethal Contraindication

Classic board lethal error: Beta-blockade before alpha-blockade eliminates beta-2 mediated vasodilation in skeletal muscle vascular beds, leaving pure, unopposed alpha-1 stimulation. This precipitates fatal hypertensive crises and acute pulmonary edema.

Pediatrics Neonatology & Therapeutics #PED-2911
schedule 3 Days Ago Resolved in Mock #4

Empirical Sepsis Regimen in Jaundiced Neonates (<28 Days)

A 12-day-old neonate with physiological jaundice is admitted for suspected late-onset sepsis. Which third-generation cephalosporin is explicitly contraindicated due to risk of kernicterus?

close Selected Distractor Option C

"Cefotaxime (due to immature biliary excretion kinetics)"

check_circle Correct Answer Option A

"Ceftriaxone (displaces unconjugated bilirubin from albumin binding sites)"

published_with_changes
Trap Type: Negative Drug Substitution Cue

Cefotaxime is the agent of choice in neonates. Ceftriaxone avidly binds to serum albumin and unseats unconjugated bilirubin, markedly increasing free indirect bilirubin capable of crossing the blood-brain barrier into basal ganglia (Kernicterus).

The Rule of Halves: Adrenal Crisis & Addisonian Hyponatremia

In Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's), both mineralocorticoid AND glucocorticoid deficiencies cause severe hyperkalemia with hyponatremia. In contrast, Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency (Pituitary ACTH failure) presents with normal aldosterone levels, so serum potassium remains entirely normal.

Source: Harrison Endocrine Ch. 379
